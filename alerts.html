    Weather Alerts & Warnings

    What a winter storm warning, tornado watch, flood watch or heat advisory really means — and what to do when one is issued for your city.

    Weather Alerts, Warnings & Advisories Explained

    Weather alerts are official warnings that dangerous weather is coming or already happening. In the United States they are issued by the National Weather Service, and every city page on Weather Tomorrow links to the live forecast so you can check whether an alert is active for your area. This guide explains the main winter weather warnings, watches and advisories in plain words: what each alert means, how serious it is, and what you should do when you see one.

    The most important difference is between a watch and a warning. A watch means the weather could turn dangerous — get ready. A warning means the dangerous weather is happening now — take action immediately. An advisory sits in between: the weather will make life difficult, but it is not expected to be life-threatening. Once you know that, every alert on this page is easy to understand.

    Winter Storm Warning

    A winter storm warning means a significant winter storm is happening now or will begin very soon, and it will make travel dangerous. Heavy snow, sleet, or freezing rain is expected, with snow totals that can shut roads and knock out power. When a winter storm warning is issued for your area, stay off the roads if you can, keep extra food, water and blankets at home, and check the forecast again before you travel.

    Winter Storm Watch

    A winter storm watch means a significant winter storm is possible in the next day or two, but forecasters are not yet certain it will hit your area. It is an early heads-up: a winter storm watch is the time to prepare, not the time to panic. Check your supplies, plan your travel, and watch the forecast — a watch often becomes a warning if the storm track firms up.

    Tornado Watch

    A tornado watch means the weather conditions are right for tornadoes to form in your area, usually over the next few hours. It does not mean a tornado has been seen — it means you should stay alert. When a tornado watch is issued, keep a phone or radio nearby, know where your safe place is, and be ready to move quickly if the watch is upgraded to a warning.

    Tornado Warning

    A tornado warning means a tornado has been spotted or detected on radar, and it is happening now. This is the most serious tornado alert: move to safety immediately. Go to the lowest floor of a sturdy building, to a small interior room away from windows, or to a basement if you have one. Never stay in a car or a mobile home during a tornado warning.

    Severe Thunderstorm Warning

    A severe thunderstorm warning means a thunderstorm is producing damaging winds of 58 miles per hour or more, hail one inch or larger, or a tornado. Move indoors, stay away from windows, and do not drive through flooded roads. If you are outside, get to a sturdy building or a hard-topped car and wait for the storm to pass.

    Flood Watch

    A flood watch means flooding is possible in your area because of heavy rain, melting snow, or a dam or levee problem. It is the time to prepare: move valuables to higher ground, know your evacuation route, and never drive into water of unknown depth. A flood watch often comes before a flash flood warning when rain keeps falling on already wet ground.

    Flash Flood Warning

    A flash flood warning means flash flooding is happening now or will begin within minutes, and it is extremely dangerous. Flash floods can sweep away cars and people in seconds, and most flood deaths happen in vehicles. Never drive through flooded roads — turn around, don't drown. Move to high ground immediately if water is rising around you.

    Winter Weather Advisory

    A winter weather advisory means winter weather that is not quite severe enough for a warning is still expected, and it can make travel difficult. Light snow, freezing drizzle, or a mix of rain and snow can make roads slippery and slow your journey. Winter weather warnings is the general term for the whole family of winter alerts, and an advisory is the lowest level: be careful, allow extra time, and keep your distance from other vehicles.

    Wind Advisory

    A wind advisory means strong winds are expected that can make driving difficult, blow down tree branches, and knock out power. Secure loose outdoor items, be careful driving high-sided vehicles and trailers, and watch for falling branches. If you live in an area with frequent wind advisories, keep a torch and charged phone ready in case the power goes out.

    Heat Advisory

    A heat advisory means dangerously hot weather is expected, with high temperatures and humidity that can cause heat exhaustion or heatstroke. Drink water regularly, stay in the shade or indoors during the hottest hours, and never leave children or pets in a parked car. Check on elderly neighbours and anyone working outdoors, and know the signs of heatstroke: confusion, hot dry skin, and fainting need emergency help.

    Weather Alerts — Stay Safe in Severe Weather

    Weather alerts exist for one reason: to keep you safe. A warning is not a suggestion — it is a signal that dangerous weather is happening and you should act. The best time to learn what each alert means is before you need it, not during a storm. Read the sections above, know where your safe place is at home and at work, and keep a charged phone and a torch ready. When an alert appears for your city, check the live forecast on your city page, follow the advice in this guide, and share the warning with family and neighbours who may not have seen it.
