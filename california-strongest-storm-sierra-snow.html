    California's Strongest Storm

    Heavy Sierra Nevada snow, whiteouts, Bay Area rain and strong winds — California's biggest storm of the season, powered by MET Norway.

    What to expect across California — and how to stay safe.

    By Ahsan Ali, Founder, Weather Tomorrow · 20 November 2026

    California's Strongest Storm of the Season

    California has already had an active run of storms this November, but forecasters say those were only the warm-up. The next system is the big one: ABC10 has issued a Weather Impact Alert for the strongest storm of the season, and it brings the full set — heavy rain in the lowlands, strong winds along the coast, and heavy snow with whiteout conditions in the Sierra Nevada. If you live in California, or you are planning to travel there this week, this is the storm to take seriously.

    The pattern is familiar, but the scale is not. Storm after storm has crossed California in recent days, each one adding moisture to the ground and the air. This system is stronger than the ones before it, and it arrives while the ground is already wet — which means rain runs off faster, rivers rise quicker, and the mountains pile up snow faster. That combination is what turns an ordinary November storm into the strongest of the season.

    Sierra Nevada Braces for Heavy Snow and Whiteouts

    The Sierra Nevada is where this storm will hit hardest. Forecasters expect heavy snow across the range, and FOX Weather reports the Sierra is bracing for whiteouts — the dangerous mix of falling snow and strong wind that can cut visibility to almost nothing in minutes. In a whiteout, you cannot see the road, the edge of the road, or the car in front of you. That is why mountain passes are the first places to close when a storm like this arrives.

    Snow in the Sierra is good news in the long run — it builds the snowpack that California depends on for water in spring and summer. But in the short run, it is a travel problem. A pass that is open in the morning can be closed by the afternoon. Anyone planning to cross the Sierra this week should check road conditions before leaving, carry chains, and be ready to turn back. The snow itself is not the only risk: strong wind can pile snow into deep drifts on the road even after the snowfall eases.

    If you are staying in the mountains, the advice is simple. Stock up before the storm arrives, keep your phone charged, and do not try to drive through a whiteout to get somewhere. Wait it out. The storm will pass, the ploughs will clear the roads, and the snow will still be there to enjoy afterwards.

    Bay Area Rain and Strong Winds

    In the Bay Area, the storm arrives as rain — heavy at times — along with strong winds. The First Alert forecast for Wednesday warned of exactly that: a storm moving through the Bay Area with bursts of heavy rain and gusts strong enough to bring down branches and cause local power cuts. Rain that falls hard in a short time is the kind that floods low-lying roads and underpasses, even in a city used to winter rain.

    The Bay Area has already had rounds of rain this month, so drains and soil are holding plenty of water. When the next band of heavy rain arrives on ground that is already saturated, water has nowhere to go but onto the roads. Drivers should allow extra time, avoid flooded underpasses — never drive through water when you cannot see the road beneath it — and keep an eye on the hourly forecast, which shows when the heaviest rain is expected. Check the live forecast on the San Francisco weather page for today, tonight and tomorrow.

    What About Southern California?

    Southern California gets its share of this system too. In San Diego, forecasters expect rain, wind, surf and thunderstorms to return on Thursday. Thunderstorms in Southern California can drop a lot of rain in a very short time, which is why streets and low-lying areas flood so quickly there. The surf will also be rough, so beachgoers should stay out of the water while the storm passes.

    Los Angeles sits between the two centres of the storm — the Sierra snow to the north and the San Diego thunderstorms to the south. The city can expect rain and wind from the same system, with the heaviest rain most likely in the hills and valleys around the city. Check the live forecast on the Los Angeles weather page and the San Diego weather page for the latest timing, and read our alerts guide on the Weather Alerts page to understand what each warning means.

    Why This Storm Is Stronger Than the Last Ones

    A storm does not need a special name to be dangerous — it needs moisture, wind and timing, and this one has all three. The recent run of storms has loaded the atmosphere with moisture, so each new system has more water to work with than the one before. The wind field is strong enough to push that moisture hard against the mountains, which is what produces the heaviest rain on the coastal side and the heaviest snow in the Sierra. And the timing is bad: it arrives while the ground is already wet, so flooding and mudslides become more likely than they would be after a dry spell.

    This is also a reminder that a "strongest storm of the season" in November is not unusual for California — the state gets its biggest storms in late autumn and winter. What matters is not the label, but the preparation. The people who get through these storms best are the ones who check the forecast early, change their travel plans before the weather does it for them, and take the warnings seriously while there is still time to act.

    How to Stay Safe in the Storm

    If you are in California this week, a few simple steps will keep you safe. First, check the forecast every morning — the forecast is recalculated every hour from fresh model data, so the morning check is the one to trust. Second, do not drive through flooded roads or into a whiteout; turn around and wait. Third, if you must travel over the Sierra, carry chains, warm clothes, food and water, and a charged phone. Fourth, secure anything loose outside your home before the wind arrives — garden furniture, bins and decorations become hazards in strong gusts. And finally, keep your phone charged so you can check the hourly forecast and receive alerts if conditions change.

    For people in the mountains, the snow will be deep and the wind will be strong. Stay indoors during the worst of it, and if you lose power, use torches rather than candles and keep heaters away from anything that can burn. For people on the coast, the danger is water: heavy rain, high surf and flooded roads. Do not walk or drive through flood water — even shallow-looking water can hide a washed-out road or a strong current.

    California's Strongest Storm — The Bottom Line

    California's strongest storm of the season is here, and it is a full-state event: heavy snow and whiteouts in the Sierra Nevada, heavy rain and strong winds in the Bay Area, and rain, wind, surf and thunderstorms in San Diego. The storm is stronger than the ones that came before it because it arrives on ground that is already wet, with plenty of moisture in the air. Check the live forecast for your city — San Francisco, Sacramento, Los Angeles or San Diego — look at the hourly breakdown, and plan around the worst hours. Stay safe, stay dry, and let the storm pass.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the strongest storm of the season in California?

    It is the November storm system that forecasters have flagged with a Weather Impact Alert — the strongest storm California has had so far this season. It brings heavy snow and whiteouts to the Sierra Nevada, heavy rain and strong winds to the Bay Area, and rain, wind, surf and thunderstorms to Southern California.

    Will there be snow in the Sierra Nevada?

    Yes. Heavy snow is expected across the Sierra Nevada, with whiteout conditions where strong wind combines with falling snow. Mountain passes may close, and travel over the Sierra will be difficult or impossible during the worst of the storm. Check road conditions before travelling.

    Is it safe to drive over the Sierra passes?

    Not during the worst of the storm. Whiteouts can cut visibility to almost nothing, and snow can pile into deep drifts on the road. If you must travel, carry chains, check conditions before you leave, and be ready to turn back. If a pass is closed, do not try to find another way around.

    Will the Bay Area get heavy rain?

    Yes. Rain is expected in the Bay Area, heavy at times, along with strong winds. Check the live forecast on the San Francisco weather page for the hourly breakdown, which shows when the heaviest rain is expected.

    Will it rain in Los Angeles and San Diego?

    Yes. Los Angeles can expect rain and wind from the same system, and San Diego can expect rain, wind, surf and thunderstorms returning on Thursday. Check the Los Angeles weather page and the San Diego weather page for the latest timing.

    How long will the storm last?

    The storm moves through California over several days, with the heaviest rain, snow and wind arriving in bands rather than all at once. The forecast is updated every hour, so check your city's weather page each morning for the latest timing — the hourly breakdown shows exactly when the worst weather is expected.
