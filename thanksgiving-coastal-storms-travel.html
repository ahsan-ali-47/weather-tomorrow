    Thanksgiving Travel Faces Storms on Both US Coasts

    Coastal storms on both sides of the United States threaten Thanksgiving journeys, with flooding in Florida, snow in the mountains and a new storm aimed at New Jersey.

    What is happening, who is affected — and how to travel safely through the holiday week.

    By Ahsan Ali, Founder, Weather Tomorrow · 22 November 2026

    Two Coasts, One Difficult Thanksgiving Week

    Millions of Americans are about to travel for Thanksgiving, and this year the weather is making the journey difficult on both sides of the country at once. Fox Weather reports that storms on the East and West coasts are forecast to disrupt Thanksgiving travel for millions of people, while CBS News describes devastating storms hammering both coasts, with epic flooding and gusty winds in Florida. It is the combination that makes this week stand out: there is no quiet corner of the country to route around, because the trouble stretches from Florida to New Jersey in the east and from California to Montana in the west.

    The timing is what turns bad weather into a travel crisis. Thanksgiving week is one of the busiest travel periods in the United States, with crowded airports, packed highways and fixed dates that cannot easily move. A storm on an ordinary weekend is an inconvenience; the same storm in Thanksgiving week means missed flights, long delays and dangerous roads for people who have to travel anyway. That is why forecasters are urging travellers to watch the forecast closely and to build extra time into every journey this week.

    The BBC adds another ingredient to the mix, reporting a rain warning for Sunday as cold weather persists, after temperatures fell well below freezing in many areas on Friday night. Cold air, coastal rain and mountain snow are all in play at the same time, and each one affects a different kind of journey. Drivers face flooded roads in the east and snow in the interior, while flyers face delays wherever low cloud and strong winds sit over busy airports.

    Florida: Flooding, Tornadoes and More Rain

    Florida has already had the worst of the flooding, and the rain is not finished yet. A forecast for Orlando from meteorologists T.J. Springer and Brooks Garner says the major flooding is over but the rain is not quite done, with scattered showers and storms expected today before brighter weather arrives on Sunday. Check the live forecast on the Orlando weather page for the latest timing, and do not assume that drier weather in one part of the state means dry roads everywhere.

    The flooding threat is expected to escalate in waterlogged places, according to Fox Weather, and Florida has also seen the week's most violent weather. The National Weather Service confirmed that a tornado was located over Kings Point in Florida, and Fox Weather reported a tornado warning after a funnel cloud loomed over a neighbourhood in Boynton Beach on Saturday evening. A tornado warning means people in the warned area should move to an interior room on the lowest floor of a sturdy building and stay away from windows until the warning passes.

    Further up the Gulf coast, the picture is calmer but still unsettled. FOX 13 meteorologist Dave Osterberg says Saturday in Tampa will bring early clouds, then sunshine and perhaps a shower, with a high temperature of 77 degrees. That is a reminder that Florida's weather varies sharply from county to county this weekend: one city can be drying out while another is still under floodwater. If you are in Florida, keep following local guidance and check the live forecast on the Miami weather page as well as the Orlando weather page.

    New Jersey and the Northeast: A Coastal Storm Takes Aim

    While Florida deals with the aftermath, a new coastal storm is taking aim at the Northeast. NJ.com reports that the storm is on track to bring heavy rain to New Jersey this weekend, along with gusty winds, dangerous surf and rip currents, although the full impacts remain uncertain. Regardless of whether the system develops subtropical or tropical characteristics, it is expected to produce strong winds and hazardous seas along the coast.

    The uncertainty matters. A coastal storm that tracks fifty miles further east would spare the coast the worst of the rain, while a track fifty miles further west would drag the heaviest weather over crowded cities and the busy I-95 corridor. That is why the forecast for the Northeast will keep changing through the weekend, and why travellers should check the morning forecast rather than relying on one issued days earlier. Check the live forecast on the New York weather page and the Philadelphia weather page for the latest rain timing.

    There is more to watch beyond this weekend. CNY Central reports gorgeous late-November weather for part of the weekend, but says forecasters are watching two nor'easters between now and around Thanksgiving. A nor'easter is a powerful coastal storm that can bring heavy rain, strong winds and coastal flooding to the Northeast, and two of them in one holiday week would keep pressure on travel for days. Check the Boston weather page for the New England outlook.

    California and the West: Rain, Snow and a Bay Area Storm

    The West Coast has its own storm to deal with, and it arrives right on Thanksgiving. CBS News reports that the Bay Area will see a significant storm during Thanksgiving, according to meteorologist Darren Peck, and the same system is expected to bring heavy rain and mountain snow across Northern California. Check the live forecast on the San Francisco weather page and the Los Angeles weather page for rain timing and wind.

    KCRA reports that Sunday and Wednesday are Weather Impact Days in Northern California because rain and snow are expected to cause travel issues, with a full breakdown of the best and worst days to drive. The message for drivers is simple: the days around Thanksgiving are not equal, and shifting a journey by a day can mean the difference between a wet drive and a dangerous one. Check the Sacramento weather page if your route crosses the Central Valley or heads into the mountains.

    When both coasts are stormy in the same week, the whole travel network feels it. Flights delayed in the Northeast can leave aircraft and crews out of place for later departures in the West, and there are few spare seats at Thanksgiving to absorb the disruption. If you are flying this week, watch your airline's app for rebooking options early, and keep essentials and chargers in your hand luggage in case of a long wait.

    Snow and Cold Across the Interior

    Inland, the story is snow. NBC Montana reports accumulating snowfall and travel impacts into Saturday, with Winter Weather Advisories in effect through Saturday morning and snow showers becoming more widespread across northwest Montana. Mountain roads can turn slick quickly when snow falls on cold ground, and drivers heading into high country should check road conditions, slow down and keep a greater distance from the vehicle ahead. Check the Denver weather page for the outlook across the interior West.

    Iowa sits between the two storm systems, with a cooler, showery weekend of its own. KCCI reports morning clouds, then showers developing later in the day and lasting into the night, with some thunder possible and a high of 65 degrees, before a cooldown arrives. Thunder with November rain is a sign of how much energy is in the atmosphere this week, and it is worth taking seriously: if you hear thunder, get inside, and never shelter under a tree in an open area.

    The cold is not only a mountain problem. The BBC reports that temperatures dropped well below freezing in many areas on Friday night, and cold air makes every other hazard worse: wet roads can freeze overnight, snow is harder to clear, and anyone travelling early in the morning should expect icy patches even where the afternoon was merely wet.

    What This Means for Thanksgiving Travel

    Put the pieces together and the picture is clear: this is a week for patience and flexibility. Fox Weather expects coastal storms to disrupt travel for millions, CBS News warns that stormy weather could hamper Thanksgiving travel, and the affected regions cover nearly every major travel corridor — Florida, the Northeast, the Bay Area, Northern California and the northern Rockies. No single forecast covers all of it, so travellers need to check conditions for each leg of their journey, not just their destination.

    If you can choose your travel day, choose carefully. In Northern California, KCRA has flagged Sunday and Wednesday as Weather Impact Days; in the Northeast, the weekend coastal storm and the nor'easters around Thanksgiving are the key threats; in the Bay Area, the significant storm falls on Thanksgiving itself. Where a journey can move by a day, moving it away from the worst weather is the single most effective step. Where it cannot move, leave early, allow generous extra time, and have a backup plan for delays.

    For drivers, the practical rules are straightforward: check the forecast on the morning you travel, keep fuel topped up, carry water, snacks, a torch and a phone charger, and let someone know your route and expected arrival time. For flyers, check in online, arrive early, and monitor departure boards before leaving for the airport. And whatever your plans, remember that forecasts will keep changing: a forecast issued on Saturday may look different by Monday, because the coastal storm's track is still being refined.

    How to Stay Safe in Floods and Winter Weather

    Floodwater is the most dangerous part of this week's weather. Never drive through a flooded road, even if the water looks shallow: it can hide a washed-out surface or a strong current, and a small amount of fast-moving water can sweep a vehicle away. If you meet floodwater, turn around and find another route. At the coast, stay off beaches, piers and rock walls while the surf is high, because large waves can sweep across a beach without warning and rip currents grow much stronger during a coastal storm — the same dangerous surf and rip currents that NJ.com warns about this weekend.

    In snow country, slow down and smooth out every input: gentle braking, gentle steering and a much bigger gap to the vehicle ahead. If your vehicle starts to slide, ease off the accelerator and steer where you want to go rather than braking hard. Keep headlights on in falling snow, and if conditions get too bad, pull fully off the road in a safe place and wait for ploughs rather than pressing on. Winter Weather Advisories mean that travel impacts are expected, so take them as a warning to change plans, not as background noise.

    At home, prepare for disruption before it arrives: charge phones and torches, secure garden furniture and bins that can blow around in strong gusts, and move valuables and documents to higher floors if your area is under a flood warning. If officials tell you to evacuate, leave promptly and never wait to see how bad it gets. Read our guide on the Weather Alerts page to understand what each warning means.

    What Happens Next

    The next few days will sharpen the forecast. The coastal storm aimed at New Jersey will show its hand as it approaches the coast, the nor'easters watched by CNY Central will come into clearer focus, and the Bay Area storm will firm up its timing for Thanksgiving. Meanwhile, NOAA's Eastern North Pacific tropical weather discussion continues to monitor conditions in the eastern Pacific, a reminder that the tropics stay active even in late November.

    Check the live forecast every morning this week. The New York weather page, the Boston weather page, the Philadelphia weather page, the Washington DC weather page, the Charlotte weather page and the Atlanta weather page all show hourly breakdowns for the east, while the San Francisco weather page, the Los Angeles weather page, the Sacramento weather page, the Denver weather page, the Orlando weather page and the Miami weather page cover the west, the interior and Florida. Stay safe, stay patient, and give the storms room to pass.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Will coastal storms disrupt Thanksgiving travel?

    Yes. Fox Weather reports that storms on the East and West coasts are forecast to disrupt Thanksgiving travel for millions, and CBS News warns that stormy weather could hamper journeys. The worst days vary by region: the weekend coastal storm in the Northeast, Sunday and Wednesday in Northern California, and a significant storm in the Bay Area on Thanksgiving itself. Check conditions on the morning you travel and allow extra time.

    Was there a tornado in Florida?

    Yes. The National Weather Service confirmed that a tornado was located over Kings Point in Florida, and Fox Weather reported a tornado warning after a funnel cloud loomed over a neighbourhood in Boynton Beach on Saturday evening. A tornado warning means moving to an interior room on the lowest floor of a sturdy building, away from windows, until the warning passes.

    Which areas face the heaviest rain?

    Florida faces continued showers and storms after major flooding, New Jersey faces heavy rain from a coastal storm this weekend, and Northern California faces rain that has earned Sunday and Wednesday Weather Impact Day status from KCRA. The heaviest rain generally falls near the coasts, where storms draw moisture from the ocean, while the interior faces snow instead.

    Will there be snow for Thanksgiving travel?

    Yes, in places. NBC Montana reports accumulating snowfall and travel impacts into Saturday, with Winter Weather Advisories through Saturday morning, and KCRA expects snow to cause travel issues in Northern California. Mountain roads in the West and the northern Rockies are the most affected, and drivers should check road conditions and slow down.

    Is it safe to drive through a flooded road?

    No. Floodwater can hide a washed-out road or a strong current, and a small amount of fast-moving water can sweep a vehicle away. Turn around and find another route, and never drive around barriers. At the coast, stay off beaches and piers during high surf, when rip currents are much stronger.
