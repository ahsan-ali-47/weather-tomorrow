    How the Weather Changes Around the World

    The world has very different climates, and the same month can mean opposite weather in different places. When it is winter in Pakistan and Europe, it is summer in Australia and South Africa, because the southern hemisphere has its seasons reversed. When Lahore is covered in smog in November, Sydney is heading into summer. When Dubai is hot and dry, London is cool and rainy. That is why a world weather page is useful: the weather where you are tells you nothing about the weather where you are going.

    Desert countries like the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia have very hot summers, mild winters, and almost no rain for most of the year. Tropical countries near the equator, like Singapore, stay warm all year with frequent rain. Temperate countries like the United Kingdom, Germany and France have four clear seasons: a cool winter, a mild spring, a warm summer and a cool autumn. Mountain cities like Murree and Abbottabad are much colder than the plains below them, and the temperature can drop several degrees in a short drive uphill. Always check the weather for the exact city, not just the country, because the weather changes with altitude, distance from the sea, and local geography.

    Planning Travel with the Weather

    Before any journey, check three things: the weather today, the weather tomorrow, and the 10-day forecast for your destination. The weather today tells you what to pack right now. The weather tomorrow tells you what the first day of your trip will be like. The 10-day forecast shows the pattern for the whole trip, so you can see whether to pack for rain, heat, or cold. If you are travelling between seasons — for example, leaving hot Dubai for cool London — the difference can be twenty degrees or more, and packing the wrong clothes can ruin the trip.

    Rain is the detail most travellers forget to check properly. A weather app that says "40% chance of rain" tells you almost nothing. Weather Tomorrow shows rain in millimetres, so you know how much rain is really expected: 1 mm is a light shower you can ignore, 5 mm is steady rain that needs an umbrella, and 20 mm or more in a day means heavy rain and possible delays. Check the hourly forecast for your destination to see exactly which hours will be wet and which will be dry.

    Seasons, Monsoons and the Best Time to Travel

    Every region has a season when the weather is at its best and a season when it is at its worst. In Pakistan, the monsoon months bring heavy rain to Lahore, Islamabad and the northern cities, while winter brings cold mornings, fog and smog to the plains. In the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia, the best weather comes in the winter months, when the temperature is mild and pleasant, while the summer heat can be extreme. In the United Kingdom and northern Europe, summer is the warmest and driest season, while winter is cold, dark and rainy. In Japan, spring brings cherry blossoms and mild weather, while summer brings heat and humidity. In Australia, the seasons are reversed: December is summer and July is winter.

    The smartest way to plan a trip is to check the weather for your destination in the month you are travelling, not just the week you are travelling. The 10-day forecast tells you the weather for the coming days, but the climate of the place tells you what to expect in general. A city that is hot in July may be cold in January, and a city that is dry in winter may be drenched in the monsoon. Weather Tomorrow shows you the live forecast for today and the next ten days, and the country and city pages help you understand the place itself. Check both, and the weather will never catch you by surprise.
