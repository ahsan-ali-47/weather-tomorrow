    Our Meteorological Data Architecture

    Weather Tomorrow is engineered with an unwavering commitment to data integrity. Rather than relying on commercial ad-heavy aggregators or synthetic AI temperature estimators, every single metric rendered on this site is retrieved from authoritative open scientific institutes:


    • Meteorological Institute of Norway (MET Norway): Locationforecast 2.0 API provides our complete weather forecast stack, including instant temperature, barometric pressure, relative humidity, wind velocity, and hourly precipitation models. Used under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license (CC BY 4.0).
    • OpenStreetMap & Nominatim: Powers our global search and geocoding index, allowing fast lookups of any village, town, or metropolis worldwide with zero proprietary tracking. Sourced under the Open Database License (ODbL).
    • OpenAQ: Connects our platform to public ground-based air quality monitoring stations for PM2.5 and PM10 telemetry. If a location lacks physical sensors, we honestly omit the air quality card rather than displaying fabricated numbers.

    About Weather Tomorrow — Free Weather, Honest Data

    Weather Tomorrow is a free weather website for everyone, everywhere. It started with a simple idea: weather information should be free, fast, and honest. No account, no payment, no fake numbers. You open the site, search your city, and you see the real weather — the temperature, the rain in millimetres, the hourly forecast and the 10-day forecast — all from live data. The site is built and run by Ahsan Ali, the founder, from Multan, Pakistan, and it serves cities in Pakistan, the United Arab Emirates, the United States, and around the world.

    Our weather data comes from MET Norway, the Norwegian Meteorological Institute, through its free Locationforecast service. MET Norway is one of the most respected weather services in the world, and it publishes its forecast data openly so that anyone can use it. That is why a small, independent website can show the same quality of forecast as the biggest weather apps. We do not invent weather numbers, and we do not guess. If a value is not available — for example, if there is no air quality station in a city — we say so honestly instead of showing a made-up number.

    What Makes Weather Tomorrow Different

    Most weather sites show a rain percentage, which tells you almost nothing: a 50% chance of rain could mean a drizzle or a downpour. We show rain in millimetres, so you know exactly how much rain is expected. Most weather sites hide their data sources. We name ours on every page: data from MET Norway, search from OpenStreetMap, air quality from OpenAQ. Most weather sites are heavy and slow. We keep our pages light and fast, because weather is something you check in a hurry, on a slow connection, on a cheap phone — and it should just work.

    We also write in plain words. Weather should not need a dictionary. Temperature, feels-like, humidity, wind, rain and UV are explained in simple language on every page, and our blog explains the bigger topics — smog in Lahore, how rain forecasts work, how accurate the 10-day forecast is — in the same plain language. If you are a beginner, you should still understand everything on this site.

    Privacy and the Future

    Weather Tomorrow does not ask you to create an account, and it does not sell your data. The site remembers only your theme choice (light or dark) and your language on your own device. Your location is used only when you ask to search near you. That is the whole privacy story, and it is written in our privacy policy in plain words.

    The site is young and growing. New city pages are added regularly, starting with the smaller cities of Pakistan that the big weather sites ignore, because a person in Layyah or Mansehra deserves a weather forecast just as much as a person in London. A blog, air quality coverage and more cities are already live, and more is coming. If you use the site every day, you are helping it grow — and if you find a mistake, tell us through the website and it will be fixed.

    How to Use Weather Tomorrow

    Using the site is simple. Search any city in the box on the homepage, or browse the countries page to find a city by country. Every city page shows the current weather, the hourly forecast, the 7-day strip and the 10-day forecast, plus the air quality when a station is available. The hourly forecast page shows the temperature curve and rain hour by hour. The 10-day forecast page shows the week ahead. The AQI & Smog page shows the air quality. And the blog explains the weather in plain words, from smog in Lahore to how rain forecasts work. Everything is free, everything is live, and everything is honest.
