    Snowfall in Murree — Season, Forecast & Travel Guide

    Welcome to the complete guide to snowfall in Murree, the hill station in Punjab, Pakistan (Coordinates: 33.9047°N, 73.3919°E; approximate elevation: 2,291 m). Murree is the nearest place to see real snow for most people in Pakistan, which is why thousands of visitors drive up from Islamabad, Rawalpindi and Lahore every winter. This page gives you the live snow forecast for Murree, the snowfall season, the best time to visit, and practical tips for a safe snow trip — all from the MET Norway forecast model, updated every hour.

    When Does It Snow in Murree?

    Snow in Murree usually falls from late December to February, with January the most likely month. The snow arrives with winter western disturbances, the storm systems that bring rain and snow to northern Pakistan in winter. A strong disturbance can bring heavy snow that covers the town in a few hours, while a weak one may bring only rain or a light dusting on the higher ground.

    It rarely snows in Murree in November or March, although a very cold spell can bring a light fall at the edges of the season. The exact dates change every year, so the snowfall forecast matters more than the calendar: if rain is forecast while temperatures are near or below freezing, snow is likely.

    The Best Time to Visit Murree for Snowfall

    January is the best time to visit Murree for snowfall, because that is when snow is most likely and the town is at its whitest. Late December and early February are also good, especially just after a winter storm has passed and the sky has cleared.

    Plan to arrive before the storm, not during it, because roads can close while it is snowing. Weekends and public holidays are very crowded in Murree in winter, so a weekday trip is calmer and cheaper, and hotels are easier to find.

    Where to See Snow in Murree

    Mall Road is where most visitors see their first snow in Murree: snow settles on the rooftops, the shops and the pine trees along the road. Kashmir Point and Pindi Point, the two high viewpoints at either end of the town, give the classic snow views over the hills. The Patriata chairlift at New Murree, a short drive from the main town, carries visitors over snow-covered pine forest on a clear winter day.

    The best snow views come on a clear morning just after a snowfall, before the sun softens the snow and before the crowds arrive. If you want photographs, go out early.

    Road Tips — Driving to Murree in Snow

    Murree is about an hour's drive from Islamabad on the Murree Expressway, which makes it the easiest snow trip in Pakistan — but winter driving still needs care. Confirm the road is open before you leave home, and do not travel during a snowstorm. Keep the fuel tank full, and carry warm clothes, food and water in the car in case of delays on the road.

    Drive slowly on snow and ice, keep a long gap from the car in front, and avoid sudden braking. If the traffic stops in heavy snow, stay warm in the car and wait for the road to clear rather than walking far in the cold. Book your hotel in advance: in January, hotels in Murree fill fast, especially on weekends, and searching for a room after dark in the snow is no fun.

    What to Pack for Snowfall in Murree

    Pack for real cold, not just a light jacket. Nights in Murree fall below freezing in winter. Bring warm layers, a waterproof jacket, waterproof shoes or boots with grip, gloves and a warm cap. A spare pair of dry socks matters more than most people expect after walking in snow. Keep your phone charged — cold drains batteries faster, and you will need your phone for the forecast, maps and calls.
