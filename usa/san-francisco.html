    San Francisco Weather, Climate & Meteorological Overview

    Welcome to the San Francisco weather intelligence report for San Francisco, California, United States (Coordinates: 37.7879°N, 122.4075°W; approximate elevation: 16 m). San Francisco occupies a hilly peninsula between the Pacific Ocean and San Francisco Bay, a city famous for cool, foggy summers and mild, wet winters. Weather Tomorrow streams continuous high-resolution forecasts computed by the Norwegian Meteorological Institute (MET Norway), so residents, travelers, and meteorologists get verifiable data free from estimation bias.

    Atmospheric & Seasonal Patterns in San Francisco

    San Francisco's climate is shaped by the cold California Current offshore and by the marine layer — a blanket of cool, moist air that rolls in from the Pacific, especially in summer. The city's many hills create sharp microclimates: one neighborhood can be sunny and warm while another, a few blocks away, sits in fog. The ocean keeps temperatures in a narrow band all year, so the city rarely gets very hot or very cold, and the difference between the warmest and coldest months is smaller than in almost any other major American city.

    Summer is famously the coolest-feeling season: fog and onshore wind hold highs near 68°F (20°C), and evenings often require a jacket — the opposite of most of the country. Winter is the wet season, with rain falling mainly from November to March and highs near 57°F (14°C). Autumn, especially September and October, is often the warmest and sunniest time of year, when the fog pulls back and the city enjoys clear, warm days.
