    How to Read Your City Weather Page

    Every city weather page on Weather Tomorrow works the same way, whether the city is Lahore, Karachi, London, New York, Tokyo or Dubai. At the top you see the current weather: the temperature right now, the feels-like temperature, the humidity, the wind speed and direction, and the rain. Below that is the hourly forecast, then the 7-day forecast strip, and further down the full 10-day forecast. This guide explains each part, so you can read any city page in seconds and know exactly what the weather will do.

    Start with the current weather card. The big number is the temperature in degrees Celsius. Under it, the feels-like temperature tells you how hot or cold it really feels, after wind and humidity are counted. The four small tiles show humidity, wind, UV and the high and low for the day. Humidity tells you how damp the air is. Wind tells you how strong the air is moving and where it comes from. The UV index tells you how strong the sun is. The high and low tell you the hottest and coldest moments of the day, so you know how much the temperature will swing.

    Weather Today and Weather Tomorrow

    The two questions everyone asks are about today and tomorrow. Today's weather is the most accurate forecast there is, because today is already happening. Tomorrow's weather is almost as reliable, because tomorrow is only one day away. On the city page, today is in the current conditions and the hourly forecast, and tomorrow is the first full day in the 10-day forecast. If you are planning something for tomorrow — a journey, an outdoor event, a day of work outside — look at tomorrow's maximum and minimum temperature and its rain in millimetres. That is the whole plan in two numbers.

    Rain is shown in millimetres on every city page, not as a percentage. Millimetres tell you how much rain is really expected: 0 mm is dry, 1 mm is a light shower, 5 mm is steady rain, and 20 mm or more in a day is heavy rain. If you want to know exactly when the rain will fall, open the hourly forecast page, which shows rain hour by hour. Our guide is it going to rain tomorrow? explains how rain forecasts are made and why millimetres are more useful than percentages.

    The 7-Day Strip and the 10-Day Forecast

    Below the current weather you will see the 7-day forecast strip: seven small cards, one for each day, with the weather symbol, the maximum and minimum temperature, and the rain in millimetres. It is the fastest way to see the week at a glance. For more detail, the 10-day forecast further down the page shows the daily maximum and minimum, rain, humidity and wind for ten days. The first three days are the most accurate, days four to seven show the general trend, and days eight to ten are a rough direction. Read our full guide on how accurate the 10-day forecast is to understand why.

    Some city pages also show the air quality, with the Air Quality Index and the PM2.5 reading when a monitoring station is available. The AQI turns pollution into one number: below 50 is good, 101 to 150 is unhealthy for sensitive people, and above 300 is hazardous. In winter, cities in Punjab face heavy smog, and our smog in Lahore guide explains what it is and how to stay safe. If a city has no air quality station, the page says so honestly instead of showing a made-up number.
