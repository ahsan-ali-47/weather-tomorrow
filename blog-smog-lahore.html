    Smog in Lahore

    Why smog covers Lahore every winter, what the smog level means today, and how to stay safe.

    By Ahsan Ali, Founder, Weather Tomorrow · 6 November 2026

    What Is Smog in Lahore?

    Smog in Lahore is the thick grey haze that covers the city every winter. It is not fog. Fog is water vapour. Smog is a mix of smoke and fog: tiny particles of dust, smoke and chemicals floating in damp winter air. When you cannot see the buildings across the road in the morning, that is smog. Lahore is one of the most smog-affected cities in the world, and every year from late October to January the air turns heavy, visibility drops, and breathing becomes hard.

    The smog in Lahore today is part of a seasonal pattern, not a one-day event. It builds up over weeks, gets worse on still, cold mornings, and only clears when wind or rain washes the air. This guide explains why smog happens in Lahore, what the smog level means, and what you can do to stay safe.

    Why Does Lahore Get Smog Every Winter?

    There is no single cause of smog in Lahore. Several sources add pollution to the air at the same time, and winter weather traps it:

    Crop residue burning. After the rice harvest, farmers in Punjab burn leftover straw in the fields. The smoke drifts toward Lahore and adds a huge amount of fine particles to the air in October and November.

    Vehicles. Lahore has millions of cars, motorcycles, rickshaws and trucks. Their exhaust releases smoke and gases every day, and old vehicles without proper tuning release far more than new ones.

    Brick kilns and industry. Brick kilns around the city burn coal and other fuel, and factories add their own smoke. Construction sites add dust to the same air.

    Winter inversion. In winter the air near the ground becomes colder than the air above it. This is called a temperature inversion. Normally warm air rises and carries pollution away, but during an inversion the cold layer acts like a lid. The smoke has nowhere to go, so it sits over the city.

    Low wind. Lahore's winter winds are often very slow. Without wind, the smog does not blow away, so each day adds more pollution to the same trapped air.

    Smog in Lahore Today — How to Check the Smog Level

    The best way to know the smog in Lahore today is the Air Quality Index, or AQI. The AQI turns the pollution in the air into one simple number. On Weather Tomorrow you can check the live AQI for Lahore on the AQI & Smog page and the Lahore weather page. Here is what the number means:

    0 to 50 — Good. The air is clean. Normal outdoor activity is fine.

    51 to 100 — Moderate. Acceptable for most people. Very sensitive people should watch for symptoms.

    101 to 150 — Unhealthy for sensitive groups. Children, older people and people with asthma or heart problems should reduce outdoor time.

    151 to 200 — Unhealthy. Everyone may feel it. Avoid long outdoor exercise.

    201 to 300 — Very unhealthy. Stay indoors as much as possible and wear a mask outside.

    Above 300 — Hazardous. Emergency conditions. Avoid going outside unless necessary.

    When people search for "smog index Lahore" or "smog level in Lahore today", this AQI number is what they are looking for. Check it in the morning before leaving home, because the smog is usually worst early in the day and improves a little by the afternoon.

    Smog Holidays and Smog Lockdowns in Lahore

    When the smog becomes severe, the Punjab government has in past years announced smog holidays for schools in Lahore and sometimes a smog lockdown: offices and markets closing early, and restrictions on outdoor activity. A "smog holiday in Lahore" usually means schools are closed for a few days until the air improves. A "smog lockdown in Lahore" is a stronger step, with more limits on movement and business hours.

    Whether schools close depends on the official smog order for that week. The Lahore High Court has also issued smog orders in past seasons, pushing the government to act against the worst polluters. If you hear about a holiday in Lahore due to smog, confirm it from the official government announcement before assuming schools are closed.

    How to Stay Safe During Smog

    You cannot control the smog, but you can reduce how much of it you breathe:

    Wear a proper mask. A simple cloth mask does very little against fine smog particles. An N95 mask filters far more of them. Wear it whenever you go outside on a smoggy day.

    Stay indoors in the morning. Smog is thickest in the early morning. If you can, delay outdoor work, walking or exercise until the afternoon, when the air is usually a little clearer.

    Keep windows closed. On heavy smog days, keep windows and doors closed and use an air purifier indoors if you have one. Avoid burning anything indoors.

    Avoid outdoor exercise. Running or playing outside during smog means breathing much more polluted air. Exercise indoors until the AQI improves.

    Drink water and watch for symptoms. If your eyes burn, your throat hurts, or you keep coughing, stay inside and rest. People with asthma should keep their inhaler close and follow their doctor's advice.

    Smog in Lahore — The Bottom Line

    Smog in Lahore is a winter problem with many causes: field burning, traffic, kilns, dust and trapped cold air. It will not disappear in one day, but knowing the smog level, checking the AQI before going out, and taking simple precautions makes a real difference. Check the Lahore weather today and the AQI & Smog page every morning during smog season, and plan your day around the air, not just the temperature.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What causes smog in Lahore?

    Smog in Lahore is caused by a mix of vehicle exhaust, crop residue burning in Punjab, brick kilns, construction dust and industrial smoke. In winter, cold air traps this pollution close to the ground, so the smog gets thick and stays for days.

    When is smog season in Lahore?

    Smog season in Lahore usually runs from late October to January, with November and December the worst months. Cool, still air and low wind let pollution build up instead of blowing away.

    Is smog in Lahore dangerous?

    Yes. Thick smog irritates the eyes, nose and throat, triggers asthma and coughing, and is worst for children, older people and anyone with breathing problems. On heavy smog days it is better to stay indoors as much as possible.

    How can I check the smog level in Lahore today?

    Open the Lahore weather page or the AQI & Smog page on Weather Tomorrow for the live air quality reading. The AQI number tells you the smog level: below 50 is good, 101 to 150 is unhealthy for sensitive groups, 151 to 200 is unhealthy for everyone, and above 300 is hazardous.

    Do schools close in Lahore because of smog?

    In past smog seasons the Punjab government has announced smog holidays for schools in Lahore when the air became very bad. Whether schools close depends on the official smog order for that week, so check the latest government announcement.
