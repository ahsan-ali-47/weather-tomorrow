    10-Day Weather Forecast

    How accurate is the 10-day forecast, which days you can trust, and how to use it wisely.

    By Ahsan Ali, Founder, Weather Tomorrow · 6 November 2026

    How Accurate Is the 10-Day Weather Forecast?

    The 10-day weather forecast is one of the most used features on any weather site. People check it before planning a wedding, a trip, a cricket match, or a week of work outdoors. But how much can you trust a forecast that looks ten days into the future? The honest answer is: the first few days a lot, the last few days much less. This guide explains why, and how to use the 10-day forecast wisely.

    On Weather Tomorrow, the 10-day forecast page shows the daily maximum and minimum temperature, rain in millimetres, humidity and wind for the next ten days. The data comes from MET Norway's weather models, which are among the best in the world. Even so, no model can see the future perfectly.

    Accuracy Day by Day

    Forecast accuracy falls as you look further ahead, and it falls in a fairly regular pattern:

    Days 1 to 3 — Very accurate. The forecast for the next three days is usually right about temperature, rain and wind. The model has fresh measurements and only a short time for the weather to change. You can plan around these days with confidence.

    Days 4 to 7 — Good for trends. The general pattern is usually right: whether it will be a hot week, a rainy week, or a cool week. But the details can shift. Rain forecast for Thursday might move to Friday, or the temperature might be two or three degrees different from the forecast.

    Days 8 to 10 — A general direction only. The last days of the forecast show the likely pattern, not a promise. A sunny day can turn cloudy, and a dry day can bring a shower. Use these days to get a feeling for the week, not to make a final plan.

    Why Does Accuracy Drop?

    The atmosphere is chaotic. A tiny change in the wind today can grow into a completely different weather pattern a week from now. Scientists call this the butterfly effect: a small disturbance can change everything downstream. No computer, however powerful, can measure the whole atmosphere perfectly, so every forecast starts with small errors, and those errors grow with time.

    Weather models also have limited detail. They divide the sky into a grid, and each grid box may be several kilometres wide. A small cloud, a hill, or a sea breeze that fits inside one box cannot be fully represented. For tomorrow, that barely matters. For ten days ahead, it matters a lot.

    That is also why the forecast changes every day. The model runs several times a day with fresh measurements from satellites, weather stations and balloons. Each run corrects the previous one. A 10-day forecast that showed rain next Monday might show sunshine after the next update. The forecast is not wrong; it is learning.

    What the 10-Day Forecast Is Good For

    Used the right way, the 10-day forecast is very useful. It tells you whether next week will be hot or cool, wet or dry, and whether a big change is coming. A farmer can see if a dry week is ahead. A traveller can see if a trip needs warm clothes. A family can pick the best day for an outdoor event, then confirm it with the hourly forecast as the day gets closer.

    The right way to use it is simple. For days 1 to 3, trust the details. For days 4 to 7, trust the trend but expect the details to move. For days 8 to 10, take only the general direction, and check again in a few days. And for any plan that truly depends on the weather, always check the forecast on the day itself.

    How MET Norway Builds the Forecast

    Weather Tomorrow's forecast data comes from MET Norway, the Norwegian weather service. Its models simulate the atmosphere over the whole planet, using measurements from satellites, aircraft, ships, weather stations and balloons. The models run many times a day, and each run produces a new forecast for every place on Earth, including every city in Pakistan.

    MET Norway publishes its data openly, which is why a small site like Weather Tomorrow can offer the same quality of forecast as the biggest weather apps. The difference is not the data; it is how clearly the data is shown. That is why every city page shows rain in millimetres instead of a vague percentage, and why the 10-day page shows the daily maximum, minimum, humidity and wind together.

    The Bottom Line

    So, how accurate is the 10-day weather forecast? Very accurate for the first three days, good for the trend in the middle, and only a general guide for the last days. Trust it in proportion to how far ahead it looks, check it again as your plan approaches, and confirm with the hourly forecast on the day. Used that way, the 10-day forecast is one of the most useful tools a weather site can offer.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How accurate is the 10-day weather forecast?

    The first 3 days of a 10-day forecast are usually very accurate. Days 4 to 7 are good for the general trend but the details can change. Days 8 to 10 show the likely pattern only, and the exact temperature or rain can still shift.

    Why does forecast accuracy drop after a few days?

    The atmosphere is chaotic: tiny changes today grow into big differences a week later. Weather models also have limited detail, so small clouds and local winds that matter in a week cannot be fully predicted today.

    Which is more accurate, the 5-day or the 10-day forecast?

    The 5-day forecast is more accurate, because it looks less far ahead. The 10-day forecast is still useful, but treat its last days as a trend rather than a promise.

    Does the 10-day forecast change every day?

    Yes. The forecast is rebuilt several times a day with fresh measurements, so each day's update can shift the outlook for next week. Checking the 10-day forecast again before a big plan is always wise.

    Where can I see the 10-day forecast for my city?

    Search your city on Weather Tomorrow and open the 10-day forecast page. It shows the daily maximum and minimum temperature, rain in millimetres, humidity and wind for the next 10 days.
