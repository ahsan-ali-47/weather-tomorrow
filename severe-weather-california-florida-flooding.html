    Severe Weather Floods California and Florida

    Millions face flooded streets as powerful storms hit both US coasts at the same time — heavy rain, strong winds and mountain snow.

    By Ahsan Ali, Founder, Weather Tomorrow · 19 November 2026

    What Happened on Both Coasts?

    Millions of people in California and Florida faced flooded streets on 18 and 19 November 2026, as powerful storms hit both coasts of the United States at the same time. Heavy rain fell on both sides of the country, strong winds moved in with the storms, and in the California mountains the rain turned to heavy snow. Streets were left under water in both states, and weather alerts stayed in place across South Florida and much of California.

    The two storms were not the same system. A Pacific storm pushed into California from the west, while a separate system brought heavy rain and flash flooding to Florida in the east. The United States is wide enough that two different storms can hit opposite coasts on the same day — and this week, that is exactly what happened.

    The Storm in California — Rain, Wind and Mountain Snow

    In California, the storm was described as the strongest of the season so far. A powerful weather system moved through the state, bringing rain that was heavy at times and strong winds along with it. In the Bay Area, forecasters warned of heavy rain and strong winds on Wednesday as the storm passed through. Check the live forecast on the San Francisco weather page or the Los Angeles weather page for the latest rain and wind.

    While rain fell on the coast and in the valleys, the Sierra Nevada mountains received heavy snow, with whiteout conditions expected. In a whiteout, falling and blowing snow makes it impossible to see the road ahead, and mountain passes can close. Anyone planning to cross the Sierra Nevada should check the forecast first and delay the trip if a whiteout is expected. The Sacramento weather page and the San Diego weather page carry the live forecast for northern and southern California.

    Flooding in Florida — Heavy Rain on Both Coasts

    Florida faced the same problem from the other side of the country. Heavy rain and flash flooding hit the state, leaving streets under water. Brevard County received 2 to 4 inches of heavy rain, and in South Florida a weather alert remained in effect across the region on Wednesday after rounds of heavy rain on Tuesday night led to significant flooding.

    Miami and the surrounding areas were under a flood watch as the rain continued. Schools in Miami-Dade County stayed open, but local officials warned residents about flooded roads. Check the live forecast on the Miami weather page or the Orlando weather page before travelling in Florida.

    A Coastal Storm Watched by the National Hurricane Center

    At the same time, the National Hurricane Center was monitoring a potential coastal storm off the Southeast coast of the United States for possible tropical development. If the storm stays weaker and follows a westerly track, the Southeast can expect soaking rain and gusty winds. Forecasters were watching it closely, because a small change in the storm's track or strength can change how much rain falls and where.

    This is the time of year when tropical systems are less common, but the Atlantic can still produce a storm in November. The National Hurricane Center issues updates as the system develops, and anyone on the Southeast coast should follow the forecast over the next few days.

    Why Two Coasts Flooded at the Same Time

    It looks strange when both coasts flood on the same day, but the reason is simple: the United States is so wide that it has its own weather on each side. California's weather comes from the Pacific Ocean, while Florida's weather comes from the Atlantic Ocean and the Gulf of Mexico. A storm over the Pacific has nothing to do with a storm over the Atlantic — they are separate systems, driven by separate winds.

    What made this week unusual is the timing. The Pacific storm reached California just as a separate system brought heavy rain to Florida, so both coasts were dealing with flooding at once. The jet stream — the fast river of air high above the ground that steers storms — was lined up in a way that pushed a strong system into the West Coast while another system sat over the Southeast. When the jet stream sets up like that, both sides of the country can get bad weather in the same week.

    What to Do in Heavy Rain and Flooding

    Heavy rain and flooding are dangerous, and most of the danger comes from driving. Follow these rules if your area is flooding:

    1. Never drive through flooded roads. Just 15 centimetres of moving water can knock a person over, and 30 to 60 centimetres of water can float a car. Floodwater hides the road surface, so you cannot see dips, debris or washed-out sections. Turn around and find another route. Most flood deaths happen in cars.

    2. Avoid underpasses and low-lying roads. These flood first and drain last. If you see water covering the road ahead, do not enter it — find higher ground.

    3. Stay away from fast-moving water. Do not walk through flooded streets. The water can be deeper than it looks, and it can carry debris, sewage and live electrical wires.

    4. Keep your phone charged. Charge your phone before the storm arrives, and check the hourly forecast so you know when the heaviest rain is coming. If the power goes out, a charged phone is your way to get warnings.

    5. If you are caught in a flash flood, get to high ground fast. Climb to the highest floor of a building or to high ground outside. Do not stay in a basement or a ground-floor room that is filling with water.

    Severe Weather in California and Florida — The Bottom Line

    Two separate storms hit the United States at the same time this week, flooding streets in California and Florida and bringing heavy snow to the Sierra Nevada. The National Hurricane Center is also watching a possible coastal storm off the Southeast. The rain will pass, but while it lasts, the safest thing is to stay off flooded roads, check the hourly forecast, and travel only in the dry hours. Check your city's live weather page before you go out — and check again in the morning, because the forecast changes every hour.
