    Snowfall in Skardu — Season, Forecast & Travel Guide

    Welcome to the complete guide to snowfall in Skardu, the mountain town in Gilgit-Baltistan, Pakistan (Coordinates: 35.2871°N, 75.6553°E; approximate elevation: 2,255 m). Skardu is one of the coldest and snowiest towns in Pakistan, set between the Karakoram mountains at the meeting of the Indus and Shigar rivers. This page gives you the live snow forecast for Skardu, the snowfall season, the best time to visit, and practical tips for a safe winter trip — all from the MET Norway forecast model, updated every hour.

    When Does It Snow in Skardu?

    Snow in Skardu usually falls from December to February, and the town can stay snow-covered into March. Because Skardu is much colder than Murree, snow arrives earlier, lasts longer, and falls more heavily in mid-winter. Winter storms from the west bring the snow, and a strong storm can cover the whole valley in a deep white blanket.

    Unlike Murree, where snow comes in short spells, Skardu in January and February is a proper winter town: snow stays on the ground for weeks, the lakes freeze, and night temperatures fall far below freezing. The exact dates change every year, so the snowfall forecast matters more than the calendar.

    The Best Time to Visit Skardu for Snowfall

    January and February are the best months to visit Skardu for snowfall, when snow is most likely and the valley is at its whitest. Late December is also good, especially just after a winter storm has passed and the sky has cleared.

    Winter travel to Skardu needs more planning than a Murree trip. The town is remote, the weather is much colder, and fewer hotels are open in deep winter — so book a hotel with proper heating well in advance, and keep an extra day in your plan in case snow or low cloud delays the road or your flight.

    Where to See Snow in Skardu

    Skardu town itself is beautiful under snow, with the mountains rising straight above the rooftops. The frozen lakes around the town are the classic winter views: Shangrila Lake (Lower Kachura) and Upper Kachura Lake both freeze in winter and sit in snow-covered valleys a short drive from town. The Shigar valley, with its old fort and cold desert, is also striking in snow.

    The best snow views come on a clear morning just after a snowfall. Dress warmly and go out early — the light is best then, and the snow is still fresh before the day's footprints and traffic mark it.

    Road & Travel Tips — Reaching Skardu in Winter

    Skardu is reached by road from Gilgit and by flights to Skardu Airport. In winter, both can be disrupted by snow and low cloud, so check the road and your flight before travelling, and do not travel during a snowstorm. Keep an extra day in your plan for delays — winter in the Karakoram does not run to a timetable.

    On the road, drive slowly on snow and ice, keep the fuel tank full, and carry warm clothes, food and water in case of delays. In town, book a hotel with proper heating in advance, because winter heating in the mountains is not like city heating. Keep your phone charged — cold drains batteries faster, and you will need your phone for the forecast, maps and calls.

    What to Pack for Snowfall in Skardu

    Pack for serious cold. Night temperatures in Skardu fall well below freezing, and daytime temperatures often stay below freezing in January and February. Bring heavy winter layers, a down or heavily insulated jacket, thermal innerwear, waterproof boots with grip, gloves and a warm cap. Pack warm clothes for indoors as well, because heating is not always as strong as in a city hotel. A spare pair of dry socks matters more than most people expect after walking in snow.

    Snowfall in Skardu usually runs from December to February, with snow often still on the ground in March, and January and February are the best months to visit. Skardu is colder, snowier and more remote than Murree, so plan carefully: book heated accommodation in advance, keep an extra day for delays, and pack for serious cold.
