    Snowfall in Naran — Season, Forecast & Travel Guide

    Welcome to the complete guide to snowfall in Naran, the mountain town in the Kaghan Valley, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan (Coordinates: 34.9061°N, 73.6507°E; approximate elevation: 2,409 m). Naran is one of the snowiest towns in Pakistan: it sits high in the valley below Babusar Top, and in winter the whole valley turns white. This page gives you the live snow forecast for Naran, the snowfall season, the best time to visit, and practical tips for a safe winter trip — all from the MET Norway forecast model, updated every hour.

    When Does It Snow in Naran?

    Snow in Naran usually falls from late November to March, and the heaviest snow usually comes in January and February. Because Naran sits at about 2,409 metres, it is colder than Murree, so snow arrives earlier and stays longer on the ground. Winter western disturbances bring the snow, and a strong storm can cover the whole Kaghan Valley in a deep white blanket.

    By late December the valley is usually white, and snow can still fall in March. The exact dates change every year, so the snowfall forecast matters more than the calendar: if precipitation is forecast while temperatures are near or below freezing, snow is likely.

    The Best Time to Visit Naran for Snowfall

    January and February are the best months for snowfall in Naran, when snow is most likely and the valley is at its whitest. But winter travel to Naran is for prepared travellers: many hotels close in deep winter, the road from Balakot can close in heavy snow, and the town is quiet and very cold.

    December, just after the first snow, is a gentler time to visit — the valley is already white, but the deepest cold and the heaviest storms are still to come. Whatever month you choose, confirm your hotel is open and book before you travel, and keep an extra day in your plan in case the road closes.

    Where to See Snow in Naran

    Naran town itself is beautiful under snow, with the Kunhar river running through the white valley. Lake Saif-ul-Maluk, the famous lake above the town, freezes in winter — the jeep road to the lake is usually not passable in deep snow, so visitors who go in winter generally walk up, and should only attempt it in good weather, with warm clothes and plenty of daylight left. The valley road towards Babusar Top gives the classic snow views, as far as it stays open.

    The best snow views come on a clear morning just after a snowfall, before the sun softens the snow. Go out early, dress warmly, and carry your phone charged for photographs and the forecast.

    Road Tips — Driving to Naran in Snow

    Naran is reached by the N-15 road up the Kaghan Valley from Balakot. In winter the road can close in heavy snow, so check that it is open before leaving, and do not travel during a snowstorm. The high Babusar Top route on to Gilgit-Baltistan is closed in winter, so in the cold months Naran is reached only from the Balakot side.

    Drive slowly on snow and ice, keep a long gap from the car in front, and avoid sudden braking. Keep the fuel tank full, and carry warm clothes, food and water in the car in case of delays on the road. If the traffic stops in heavy snow, stay warm in the car and wait for the road to clear rather than walking far in the cold.

    What to Pack for Snowfall in Naran

    Pack for real cold, not just a light jacket. Nights in Naran fall well below freezing in winter. Bring warm layers, a waterproof jacket, waterproof boots with grip, gloves and a warm cap. A spare pair of dry socks matters more than most people expect after walking in snow. Keep your phone charged — cold drains batteries faster, and you will need your phone for the forecast, maps and calls.

    Snowfall in Naran usually runs from late November to March, with January and February the best months to visit. Naran is colder and snowier than Murree, and winter there is quieter and harder to reach — so confirm your hotel is open, check the valley road before you leave, and pack for real cold.
